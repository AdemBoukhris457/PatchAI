How PatchAI researches at three depths

When Research is on, PatchAI doesn't search the web itself. A lead agent plans the work, sends research agents out in parallel, and writes one cited report from what they bring back. The depth you pick in the composer, Standard, Deep, or Max, decides how many agents it may send, over how many rounds, and how much each one may search and read.

This page explains that machinery from the button you click down to the search engines.

The three depths

A depth is five numbers. Two of them shape the lead agent's plan: how many research agents it may send in one round, and how many rounds it may run. Three shape each research agent: how many searches it may run, how many pages it may read, and how many good pages it should aim for before it reports back.

Multiplying them gives the ceiling for one question. The ceiling is a cap, not a target: the lead stops early when nothing is missing.

Standard is also what applies when Research is off. The lead then gets no research instructions, but if it decides on its own to send research agents, Standard's limits still cap them.

The layers

The depth travels down through eight layers, from the composer to the search engines. Each layer either carries the choice further or acts on it.

How one setting reaches every layer

The depth is stored in a Python ContextVar called requested_research. stream_events sets it when a turn starts and resets it when the turn ends, so two questions never share a depth. Everything below it reads the current value through current_depth(): the brief the lead sees, the budget line each research agent sees, and the three hard limits. Research agents run inside the lead's turn, so they inherit the same value without it being passed to them.

One research turn, step by step

  1. You turn Research on, pick a depth in the menu next to the pill, and send a question. The page adds config.research, for example "max", to the request.
  2. The chat route forwards it as research: "max". The web channel accepts it and starts the turn with that depth.
  3. On every model call, the lead agent gets the research brief for that depth, and the brief already contains the deep-research workflow. It says: plan with write_todos, call research_round with at most 5 assignments, run at least 3 rounds and at most 4, don't search the web yourself, and cite with numbered links. At Deep and Max the brief adds: use the later rounds to fill gaps, check figures that disagree, and follow leads the agents raised.
  4. The lead classifies the question as straightforward, depth-first (one topic seen from several angles), or breadth-first (several separate items), and writes its plan.
  5. It starts the first round with one research_round call. The tool starts one research agent per assignment, all at once, up to the workers that depth allows. Each assignment gives the agent an objective, a time range, and what is out of scope.
  6. Each research agent sends several searches in one reply, then reads several pages in one reply. It stops when it has the pages its budget asks for or its searches start repeating. It reports back as findings with a quote and URL each, followed by conflicts and open questions.
  7. The lead reads the findings. At Deep and Max it runs at least the brief's minimum number of rounds, and every later round covers gaps, conflicts, or leads, up to the depth's round limit.
  8. It writes the report: a direct answer, sections, a table for comparisons, and a section on what is uncertain. Every claim gets a numbered citation to a page an agent actually read.
  9. The page shows the run live in the research card, turns the numbers into citation pills, and lists the cited pages under the answer.

Rounds and agents at each depth

A round is one research_round call. The tool starts every assignment at the same time, so the agents run together whatever the model would have done on its own. The first round covers the plan. Later rounds close gaps: a missing part, two sources that disagree, or a lead an agent raised. Deep runs at least 2 rounds and Max at least 3, unless the question is one narrow fact. Hard limits and guidance

Some of the numbers are enforced by code and some are instructions the models follow. The difference matters: an instruction can be ignored, but a hard limit can't.

Enforced in code

  • Rounds per question: research_round calls are capped at 2, 3, or 4.
  • Agents per round: a round starts at most 3, 4, or 5 workers. Extra assignments are reported as not run.
  • Searches per agent: 6, 10, or 15 search_web calls.
  • Page reads per agent: 8, 15, or 20 read_web_page calls.
  • Tools per agent: research agents only get the search and read tools.
  • The throttle: slots, pacing, and retries in the search plugin.

The caps on rounds, searches, and page reads are DepthLimit middleware, built on LangChain's ToolCallLimitMiddleware. Instead of a fixed number, its run_limit is read from the current depth every time it checks. A call over the limit is refused. The workers-per-round cap is inside research_round itself.

Guidance the models follow

  • Minimum rounds: 1, 2, or 3. The brief states them; a narrow factual question may stop after one round.
  • Pages to read: at least 3, 6, or 10, from each agent's budget line, unless the topic has fewer.
  • When to stop: the lead stops when nothing is missing, and an agent stops when its searches repeat.
  • Sources: prefer official and primary pages, read every page you cite, and don't invent dates or numbers.
  • Citations: numbered links to pages an agent read, numbered by first use, with no bibliography.

The ceiling is still larger than a typical run, because each agent stops when its searches repeat. The minimum rounds keep Deep and Max from stopping after a single round.

Sharing the search engines

Search goes through ddgs, which queries ordinary search engines without an API key. Those engines refuse bursts, and some of them take the whole timeout to fail. The plugin tries Yahoo first, then Google, then the automatic list. A site: filter is rewritten into the site's name, because Yahoo returns nothing for one. A page or query fetched in the last 10 minutes is answered from memory, and a fetch already under way is shared by every agent.

What you see in the app

With Research on, the pill shows the depth, for example "Research · Max", and the menu lists each level with its search ceiling. While a run works, a card above the reply follows it through four phases, and it folds into one line when the run ends.

Changing the numbers

Every depth is one line in DEPTHS. Changing a number there changes the hard limits, the brief, and the budget line together. Only the search ceilings in the composer menu are written separately.
